CHOMP: a team-preview solver for Champions VGC Reg M-C

Both open team sheets in, a mixed strategy over the 90 bring-and-lead options out

Author: Will Hooper (willhoop) Version: 3.0.0 · 2026-10-13 Status: CHOMP v1 is the team-preview solver inside ABRA. It plays on the Pokémon Showdown ladder through ABRA's live client, ROTOM. The browser userscript that carried the CHOMP name until 2.5.1 is retired (§9).

This paper is the one home for every CHOMP figure. Every figure below names the ABRA artifact it was read from. ABRA is public: https://github.com/willhoop/abra.

Abstract

At team preview in VGC doubles each player sees both six-Pokémon sheets and secretly chooses four to bring and two of those four to lead. Under open team sheets the sheets are fully known, so the choice is a simultaneous-move, two-player, zero-sum game with 90 options per side. CHOMP scores all 90 × 90 = 8,100 option pairs with a learned estimate of the chance to win, solves the resulting matrix game exactly by linear programming, and plays a sample from the equilibrium mix. In games 2 and 3 of a best-of-three it shifts half its weight towards a best response against what the opponent brought before.

CHOMP v1 was trained on 25,008 targeted simulated games plus self-play and human games, all on ABRA's simulator MEDICHAM at release eaa5becc54eb. It passed three pre-registered gates. Against the most common human bring for each team it won 0.561 [0.518, 0.603] of games (SPRT accepted H1 at 524 games). Against the bring the human actually chose for that exact matchup it scored 0.470 [0.422, 0.519] over 400 games: no clear edge over a human who has seen the opponent. That second figure is the honest ceiling on this release.

1. The decision

Each sheet holds six Pokémon. A player brings four (C(6,4) = 15 ways) and leads two of the four (C(4,2) = 6 ways), so each side has 15 × 6 = 90 options. The order of the two leads and the order of the two back members are not separate options: in doubles both lead slots face both foes, and the back order is an in-battle switch decision (solver/chomp/options.js).

Both players choose at once. The format is Champions VGC 2026 Reg M-C with open team sheets (gen9championsvgc2026regmcbo3 on Showdown), so species, items, abilities and moves are visible on both sides. Stat spreads stay hidden.

Because the choices are simultaneous there is in general no single best option. The right object is a mixed strategy: a probability over the 90 options that an opponent cannot exploit by knowing it.

2. Method

2.1 The cell scorer

For my option a and their option b, the scorer estimates P(I win | a, b, both sheets). Its inputs are engine facts computed by MEDICHAM for every pair of Pokémon across the two sheets, both ways: the damage range times the hit chance, the KO chance at the maximum and minimum roll, a sure two-hit KO, the speed order, and a priority KO (solver/chomp/v1/features.js). These are aggregated into 22 cell features over the brought four, the leads and the whole sheet.

The model is antisymmetric by construction:

logit P(win | a, b) = τ_src · ( s(x_a, y_b) − s(y_b, x_a) + bring and lead values of the species )

so a mirror match scores exactly 1/2 and swapping the chairs gives the complement. τ_src is a learned temperature per data source. Two arms were fitted, a linear lin and a small mlp; selection was on the validation set and lin won (validation log-loss 0.6533 against 0.6550) (solver/chomp/v1/model/chomp1.metrics.json).

2.3 The solve

The scored table A (90 × 90) defines a zero-sum game. CHOMP solves

x* = argmax_x  min_y  xᵀ A y      subject to  x ≥ 0, Σx = 1

exactly as a linear program with SLOWKING, ABRA's matrix-game solver (solver/slowking/matrix.js, solveLP), and samples its pick from x*. The minimax theorem guarantees that this mix has a value no opponent mix can push lower [1, 2].

2.4 Games 2 and 3 of a best-of-three

After game 1 the opponent's previous four is known. Humans repeat their four at a rate that depends on whether they won the previous game; CHOMP reads those rates from ABRA's meta store at run time. It builds an opponent model q that puts that repeat probability on the previous four and the rest on the opponent's equilibrium mix, and plays

mix = (1 − λ) · x* + λ · BR(q),     λ = 0.5 (pre-registered)

The adjustment is small by construction: in the in-table report its gain against q was +0.0064 after an opponent win and +0.0030 after a loss (docs/_reports/2026-09-27-chomp-v1.md §4). No best-of-three arena exists, so this is not a playing-strength claim.

2.5 Speed

On 173 test pairs × 2 chairs, solving the 8,100-cell table took 18 ms on average (p90 21 ms) once the cells were scored, and the equilibrium put weight on 2.0 options on average (p90 3) (docs/_reports/2026-09-27-chomp-v1.md §4). The 18 ms is the solve only; it does not include scoring the cells.

3. How it was tested

Every gate was written into solver/chomp/v1/preregistration.json before the first targeted game was played. All arena games used engine release eaa5becc54eb, the frozen Reg M-C team pool data/team-pool-frozen-regmc, honest information, and the same in-battle agent (gen5 search at 1 s) on both sides, so only the preview differs (docs/_reports/2026-09-29-chomp-v1-landed.md §2).

Intervals are Wilson 95% [5]; gate (a) uses a game-clustered bootstrap. The SPRT [3] interval is read at a data-dependent stop and is slightly optimistic.

Each test was shown able to fail. Gate (a) with the scorer's labels shuffled reads +0.0156 [+0.0072, +0.0236], a FAIL, as it must (docs/_reports/2026-09-27-chomp-v1.md §3). solver/tests/test-chomp1.js passes 32 of 32 clauses and goes red on each of its 6 deliberate breaks (docs/_reports/2026-09-29-chomp-v1-landed.md §3).

The capability ran. CHOMP made the preview in every arena game: 200 in the screen, 538 in the SPRT (including 14 played after the stop) and 400 in gate (c), with 0 CHOMP failures (docs/_reports/2026-09-29-chomp-v1-landed.md §2).

3.1 What the gates say

  • CHOMP beats an opponent-blind bring. The human-modal bring is the most common four and lead for that sheet, whoever the opponent is. CHOMP beats it.
  • CHOMP does not clearly beat a human who has seen the opponent. Gate (c)'s point estimate is below 1/2 and its interval contains 1/2. It was pre-registered as report-only, so it did not block promotion, but it is the ceiling.
  • The human set carries little bring signal for this scorer. On human test games the scorer did not beat the baseline: Δlog-loss +0.0022 [−0.0101, +0.0147], n = 188 (docs/_reports/2026-09-27-chomp-v1.md §3). The gain is on simulated play.

5. Where it runs

  • Code: solver/chomp/ in ABRA, with the v1 model at solver/chomp/v1/model/chomp1.json.
  • Live: ROTOM, ABRA's Showdown client, asks CHOMP for the preview when its arms file says "preview": "chomp". The ladder arm solver/rotom/arms/gen5-chomp-top.json plays CHOMP v1 at preview with gen5 search in battle, on a rotation of top-rated meta teams. If CHOMP throws or returns an illegal answer, the preview falls back to the team's own recorded bring and the fallback is counted (chomp:threw, chomp:illegal).
  • Proof that the choice is sent: solver/tests/test-rotom-chomp-live.js plays the real client against a scripted local server; CHOMP's choice is sent to the battle room and is legal (13 of 13; CHANGELOG-REGMC.md 1.30.0).

5.1 The ladder, so far

Three early runs of the pipeline with CHOMP at preview, rated series only, counting only the opponent forfeits that were earned (docs/_reports/2026-10-01-earned-forfeits.md):

run series W–L mean residual S − E ± SD per series n
chomp1 9–10 −0.015 ± 0.486 19
chomptop 7–8 −0.014 ± 0.474 15
chomptopfix 12–8 +0.101 ± 0.477 20

These do not measure CHOMP. They measure the whole agent (preview and in-battle search) and every run is under the 100-series bar that ABRA sets for a ladder headline (solver/PLAN.md §3, N0). With an SD near 0.48 per series, 20 series cannot separate a residual of +0.1 from 0. No ladder rating is published here, and none will be until that bar is met. ABRA's rule is a settled rating over the last N ≥ 100 series, never the peak.

6. Limitations

  • No clear edge over human bring choice (gate (c), §3).
  • The scorer learned from simulated play on MEDICHAM. Where MEDICHAM and the real game differ, so can CHOMP.
  • Stat spreads are not modelled in v1. v1 scores with a flat spread; the v2 attempt to add them did not help (§4).
  • No best-of-three arena. The game-2 and game-3 adjustment is tested only inside its own table.
  • Not tested: v1 against v0, and CHOMP alone on the ladder (an A/B with and without CHOMP).

9. Retired: the CHOMP browser userscript (Reg M-B), versions 1.0 to 2.5.1

Until 2.5.1 this repository's CHOMP was a Tampermonkey userscript for Reg M-B on Pokémon Showdown. It scored brings by a hand-weighted, single-ply damage calculation (pairwise KO points, a weather penalty, exhaustive search over the 15 brings, leads by turn-one KO pressure) and returned one deterministic answer. Its opponent sets were inferred from usage priors, and its species data came from stale mainline tables.

Its one validation, Reg M-B, 50 of the author's own ranked games: the author's win rate was 57% when his bring matched CHOMP's and 46% when it did not, not statistically significant at that sample (docs/VALIDATION-REPORT.md). ABRA judged its bring choices to test at coin level and rebuilt CHOMP from scratch (solver/PLAN.md §2.1).

Withdrawn in 3.0.0. The old deck showed three figures that did not measure the tool: ~~1323 "highest ranking reached"~~ (the peak Reg M-B ladder rating of the author's account, not a ranking and not a CHOMP measurement), ~~154% "damage it calculates exactly"~~ (one example damage readout) and ~~0.05 s "to answer"~~ (a design goal; this repository holds no recorded timing). They are withdrawn, not restated.

The userscript code stays in app/ and engine/ as history. It is not maintained and targets a retired regulation.
